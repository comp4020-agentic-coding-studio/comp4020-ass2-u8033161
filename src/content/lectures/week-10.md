Every logic covered so far — brand experience, logistics cost,
compliance, returns economics, even the psychology of the reveal — is
optimising for something the *company* cares about: perceived value, cost,
legal exposure, refund rate. This week asks a different question, about
who can actually use the box at all.

## The OXO story

In the late 1980s, Betsey Farber's arthritis made an ordinary vegetable
peeler genuinely painful to hold and use. Rather than accept that as an
unavoidable cost of the tool, she and her husband Sam Farber worked with
the design firm Smart Design to build an ergonomic alternative. The result
— OXO's Good Grips peeler — wasn't marketed as a product for people with
arthritis. It was framed as **universal design**: accessible to and usable
by as many people as possible, on the observation that a grip designed to
work well for someone with limited hand strength tends to work better for
everyone else too. The peeler went on to win design awards and sits in the
Museum of Modern Art's permanent collection.

## Why none of this course's logics predict it

Nothing in weeks 1 through 9 optimises for this. Brand-experience logic
optimises for how opening a box makes someone feel, assuming they can open
it. Logistics-cost logic optimises for the warehouse's convenience.
Compliance logic optimises for a regulator's requirement. Returns
economics optimises for what happens after a sale, not whether the sale
itself was physically accessible. A box engineered to minimise cubic
weight and material cost can still have tape strong enough to require real
grip strength, a tear-strip too small to find without close vision, or
contents-labelling in print too small to read at a glance — none of which
shows up as a cost or compliance failure in any formula covered so far,
because none of those formulas were built to notice it.

This doesn't mean accessible design is opposed to the earlier logics.
OnTheList's tear-strip (week 1, week 9) happens to be both a compliance
feature and an accessibility win — a tool-free opening path helps everyone,
not just a customer with limited grip strength. The point isn't that these
logics conflict; it's that accessibility has been invisible to every
formula in this course so far, because nobody was asking the question it
answers.
