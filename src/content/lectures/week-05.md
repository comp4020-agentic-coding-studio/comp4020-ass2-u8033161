Every week so far has looked past a sustainability claim to find the real
cost or compliance logic underneath it. This week looks directly at a
sustainability claim that is, on its own methodological terms, hard to
fault — and asks what that does and doesn't tell you.

## The numbers

The Fibre Box Association (the US corrugated packaging trade body) publishes
life-cycle assessment data reporting: a 70-75% recycling rate for corrugated
packaging, against about 8% for polypropylene containers; roughly a 50%
reduction in per-unit greenhouse gas emissions across the industry between
2006 and 2020; a methodology following the ISO 14040/14044 life-cycle
assessment standards; conducted by an independent consultancy, Anthesis
Group; and peer-reviewed before publication.

None of that is a red flag on its own. ISO 14040/14044 is a real
international standard for how a life-cycle assessment should be scoped and
reported. A named, independent firm actually ran the numbers. The work was
peer-reviewed. This is a materially more rigorous data source than most of
what appears on the packaging you've torn down in earlier weeks.

## The question rigour doesn't answer

Here is the question a peer-reviewed, ISO-compliant methodology does not
settle: **who published it, and what do they gain if you believe it?**

The Fibre Box Association is the trade association for the corrugated
packaging industry. Its LCA data compares corrugated favourably against
polypropylene — a direct plastic competitor for exactly the packaging
formats corrugated is trying to win business from. A trade body funding and
publishing a study that favours its own member industry's material is not
evidence of bad methodology. It is evidence that "is this true" and "who
benefits from you believing it" are two different questions, and a
rigorous answer to the first does not answer the second.

This is not the same failure mode as an outright fabricated statistic. It's
a subtler one, and arguably a more common one in real packaging marketing:
correct data, real methodology, selectively commissioned and framed by an
interested party. The throughline claim survives this week largely intact —
if anything it sharpens: even the best-sourced sustainability number in
this course comes from a publisher with a direct commercial stake in the
comparison it reports.
