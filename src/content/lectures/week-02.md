A box is corrugated board before it's anything else, and corrugated board is
a flute profile before it's anything else. Flute is the wavy inner layer
between the liners — its height and frequency (flutes per foot) is most of
what separates a box that survives a warehouse from one that doesn't, and
most of what separates a cheap box from an expensive one.

## The five you'll actually meet

- **A-flute** — thickest, fewest flutes per foot, best cushioning and
  stacking strength. The flute you reach for when the thing inside is heavy
  or fragile and you're willing to pay for the thickness.
- **B-flute** — thinner, more flutes per foot, best flat-crush resistance.
  Common in retail packaging that gets stacked flat rather than dropped —
  cereal boxes, canned goods.
- **C-flute** — in between, and the industry default: roughly 80% of
  corrugated shipping containers worldwide use it. It's the flute you get
  when nobody has a specific reason to ask for something else.
- **E-flute** — thin, dense, smooth outer surface. It exists for printing,
  not protection — the flute you choose when the box itself is the
  marketing surface (see week 1's Apple case study).
- **Double-wall (e.g. BC-flute)** — two flute profiles laminated together,
  for loads a single wall can't take.

## What this does and doesn't tell you

None of the above is a sustainability decision. A thicker flute uses more
paper, ships heavier, and costs more per unit — full stop. Nobody chooses
A-flute because it's "greener" than E-flute; if anything the opposite is
true, since more material means more embodied carbon per box. Flute choice
is a straightforward trade between **protection bought** and **material and
shipping cost paid**, which is exactly the throughline claim from week 1,
now with a name attached to the trade-off instead of just a description of
one.

This also gives week 1's Apple and OnTheList cases a second read. Apple's
box likely leans E-flute or a similarly print-friendly, low-bulk board —
consistent with brand-experience logic, since a thick A-flute box would add
shipping weight without adding to the reveal. A liquid product like
OnTheList's needs enough wall strength to survive a leak test (week 3)
without over-speccing the board for a low-value item — compliance logic and
cost logic pulling in the same direction, for once.
