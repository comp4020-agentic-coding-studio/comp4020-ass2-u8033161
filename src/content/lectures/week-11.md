No new source this week. Every week since week 2 has isolated one
constraint at a time — that was a deliberate simplification for teaching,
not how packaging decisions actually get made. This week removes the
simplification.

## The brief

Pick one real product currently sold online — not Apple, lululemon, or
OnTheList, since those cases are already worked. Redesign its packaging
while holding six constraints from earlier in the semester simultaneously:

- **Flute and board choice** (week 2) — what material and structure, and
  what it costs.
- **Cubic-weight cost** (week 4) — what your outer dimensions actually
  bill against, using the real formula.
- **Sustainability-claim honesty** (week 5) — if your redesign makes any
  environmental claim, it has to survive the same publisher-interest test
  applied to the Fibre Box Association's own data.
- **Returns economics** (week 7) — does your redesign change the real
  return rate or reverse-logistics cost for this product category, and in
  which direction?
- **Compliance** (week 9) — if your product could plausibly trigger a
  dangerous-goods declaration or similar requirement, your redesign has to
  survive it, not just look compliant.
- **Accessible design** (week 10) — who can actually open what you've
  designed, and does your redesign make that better or worse than the
  original?

## Why holding all six at once is the hard part

Isolated, each constraint has a fairly clean answer: smaller is cheaper on
cubic weight; thicker board is safer but costs more; a tool-free opening
helps accessibility. Held together, they pull against each other. A
smaller box lowers freight cost but may need denser padding to hold the
same ISTA-relevant protection, which raises material cost. A tool-free
tear-strip helps accessibility, but a tear-strip weakens exactly the kind
of structural integrity a fragile or heavy item needs. A sustainability
claim that's true and defensible might cost more to substantiate than one
that's vague and unverifiable — the cheap option and the honest option
aren't always the same option. None of this has a single right answer;
the deliverable is a defensible one, with the trade-offs stated rather
than hidden.
