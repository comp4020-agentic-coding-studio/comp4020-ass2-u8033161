This is the diagnostic exercise week 6's lecture and Midterm Workshop lab
walked you through, written up properly and marked. It's the same task —
pick a subject, apply real frameworks to it, name the logic it optimises
for — but here the standard is a calculation or a specific applied
critique, not a plausible-sounding paragraph.

## The brief

> Diagnose which of week 1's three design logics your subject actually
> optimises for, and prove it with at least two real numbers or a real
> critique from weeks 2-5 — not a description of what the packaging looks
> like.

Pick one subject: Apple, lululemon/Patagonia, or OnTheList from week 1, or
your own week 1 teardown package if you'd rather work from something you
physically measured. Whichever you choose, the diagnosis has to survive
contact with at least two of: a real flute/board identification and its
cost implication (week 2), a real cubic-weight calculation against that
subject's actual or estimated dimensions (week 4), or a genuine
publisher-interest critique of a sustainability claim it makes, if it
makes one (week 5). A diagnosis that only restates week 1's description of
the case in different words, without running a number or a critique
against it, hasn't done the assignment.

State your conclusion plainly — which logic, and why — before you show
your working. The reader should know your answer in the first sentence and
your evidence in the rest of the page.

## What you submit

A written diagnosis, 600-1000 words, as a single Markdown or PDF file.
Show your working for any calculation (the dimensions or estimates you
used, the formula, the result) rather than just stating a final number —
a number without its working can't be checked, and this course's own
sourcing rule applies to your work too (see [Policies](/policies/)).

Declare which subject you chose and which frameworks you applied in your
opening paragraph, so the marker can check the right things without
guessing.
