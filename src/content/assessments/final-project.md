This is the assignment week 11's studio built toward and week 12's Final
Defense presented: one real product, redesigned under six constraints held
simultaneously, written up and defended.

## The brief

> Redesign a real product's packaging so that it holds flute/board choice,
> cubic-weight cost, sustainability-claim honesty, returns economics,
> compliance, and accessible design in tension at once, and argue your
> trade-offs rather than hide them.

Choose one product currently sold online that isn't one of this course's
own worked cases. Realistic scope: this is a redesign of packaging, not a
new product, a new supply chain, or a rebrand — the six constraints from
week 11 are the whole assignment, and a submission that adds scope beyond
them (a marketing plan, a manufacturing process redesign) is spending
effort the brief doesn't ask for.

The strongest submissions state plainly which constraint they traded away
or downweighted, and why that trade-off was the right call for this
specific product — not which constraints they satisfied, since satisfying
all six equally well is rarely the honest answer. A submission that claims
to have optimised every constraint without cost is more likely to have
examined one loosely than solved a genuinely hard problem.

## What you submit

A written redesign document (packaging description or sketches, the real
formulas and critiques applied per constraint, and your stated
trade-offs), plus your week 12 Final Defense presentation. The written
document should read as a standalone account of the redesign — a marker
who wasn't in week 12's session should be able to follow your reasoning
from the page alone, then check it against what you actually defended.
