## Before the lab

Pick a product category from Monday's lecture (apparel, footwear,
electronics, beauty, jewelry) — your own week 1 package's category is
fine if it fits one of these.

## In the lab

- **Estimate the returns** — as an illustrative exercise, not a real sales
  figure, assume a stated hypothetical monthly order volume for your
  chosen category (pick a round number and label it as invented). Apply
  that category's return rate from Monday's lecture to estimate a monthly
  number of returns.
- **Price the round trip** — using Monday's reverse-logistics cost range,
  or your own clearly-labelled illustrative estimate if your category
  isn't electronics, estimate the cost of processing those returns.
- **Decide the "keep-it" threshold** — compare that per-item processing
  cost against a stated (illustrative) retail value for your product.
  If the processing cost is close to or above the item's value, would a
  "keep-it" refund make sense here? Say why or why not.
- **Trace it back to packaging** — name one packaging or fulfilment
  decision (box size, insert design, return-label placement) that would
  shift your product's actual return rate, and identify which other cost
  from earlier weeks (week 4's freight formula, week 2's material cost)
  it would trade against.

## Afterwards

Keep your model. Week 11's redesign studio asks you to hold this
constraint — returns economics — alongside five others at once.
