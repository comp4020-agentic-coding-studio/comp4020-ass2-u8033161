## Before the lab

Bring back the package from week 1's teardown. If it's recycled already,
any corrugated box will do — the point is measuring a real one, not a
diagram.

## In the lab

- **Measuring flutes** — cut a cross-section (or find an existing tear) and
  count flute peaks against a ruler over one inch. Match your count against
  the reference numbers from Monday's lecture (A ≈ 33/ft, B ≈ 47/ft, C ≈
  39/ft, E ≈ 90/ft) to identify what you're holding.
- **Costing the choice** — for this exercise, work from an illustrative
  example figure, not a real quoted price: assume a hypothetical per-sheet
  cost difference of $0.03 between B-flute and E-flute stock at the
  quantities a small retailer orders. Using that assumption, estimate
  roughly how much flute choice alone adds to the cost of a package, and
  whether that number is large enough to explain why a retailer would care.
- **Failure cases** — as a group, name a real product category where the
  flute in your hand would fail in transit (too thin for the weight or
  fragility) and one where it would be needless overkill (too thick for
  what's inside).

## Afterwards

Keep your flute identification and your cost estimate. Week 3 asks what
"survives transit" is actually tested against, which is the other half of
the trade-off this lab only estimated.
