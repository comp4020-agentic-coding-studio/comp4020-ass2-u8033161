## Before the lab

Bring back your week 1 package. You'll need a tape measure or ruler and
something to weigh it on — kitchen or bathroom scales are fine; if you
genuinely can't weigh it, estimate from the product's listed weight and say
so.

## In the lab

- **Measure** — record the outer length, width and height of your box in
  metres (not cm — the formula wants metres), and its actual weight in
  kilograms.
- **Compute** — apply the formula from Monday's lecture: cubic weight (kg)
  = L x W x H x 250. Compare it against the actual weight and state which
  one is chargeable.
- **Diagnose** — if cubic weight came out higher, work out how much of that
  is genuinely needed (fragile contents, required cushioning per week 3)
  versus how much is unused space that a tighter box would have removed.
  If actual weight came out higher, say so — not every package is
  volume-bound, and that's also a finding.
- **Compare** — as a class, line up everyone's cubic-to-actual ratios.
  Which product categories show the biggest gap, and does that match what
  week 1 predicted about logistics-cost logic?

## Afterwards

Keep your numbers. Week 6 revisits the three week 1 case studies with
every formula so far, including this one — you'll need a real cubic-weight
comparison for at least one of them.
