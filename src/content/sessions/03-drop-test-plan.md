## Before the lab

Pick a real product you could plausibly ship — something you own, or
something you'd buy online this week. You'll test-plan it, not test it.

## In the lab

- **Scope check** — does your product fit ISTA 3A's scope at all? Under
  70kg, and one of standard / small / flat / elongated? If not, say so and
  move on; this is itself information about who this standard was written
  for.
- **Pick your elements** — from Monday's six-part procedure, choose which
  you'd actually run for this product and which you'd skip. A textbook, a
  bottle of wine, and a flat-packed poster don't need the same test plan;
  say why yours doesn't.
- **Price the failure** — for each element you kept, state in one sentence
  what a failure there would cost: a refund, a re-ship, a damaged-goods
  writeoff, a liability claim, a grounded shipment. This is the number that
  justifies running the test at all.

## Afterwards

Swap plans with another group and try to find one test element they kept
that you'd have skipped, or vice versa — and settle the disagreement with a
cost argument, not a "better safe than sorry" one. "Better safe than sorry"
is exactly the sustainability-flavoured hand-wave this course exists to
replace with a number.
