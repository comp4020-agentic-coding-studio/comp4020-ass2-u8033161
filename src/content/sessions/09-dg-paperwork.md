## Before the lab

Pick a real product category you could plausibly sell online that would
trigger a dangerous goods declaration: a fragrance or aerosol (flammable
liquid or gas), a lithium-battery device, or something similar. You don't
need the actual UN number memorised — you'll look it up.

## In the lab

- **Fill the fields** — using Monday's lecture, draft what your
  declaration would need to state: UN/ID number, proper shipping name,
  hazard class, packing group, IATA packing instruction number. Look up
  real values for your chosen product category where you can.
- **Break it on purpose** — pick one required field and imagine it's
  missing or wrong on the declaration. What actually happens: does the
  carrier refuse the shipment outright, delay it for correction, or is
  there a fine involved? Be specific about which one, not just "it would
  be bad."
- **Separate the box from the paperwork** — does anything about your
  product's retail packaging (the tear-strip, the label, the box material)
  reduce how much declaration paperwork you need, or are the two entirely
  independent problems that both have to be solved?

## Afterwards

Keep your declaration draft. Week 11's redesign studio treats compliance
as one of six constraints held simultaneously — this lab is your first
real attempt at pricing just this one.
