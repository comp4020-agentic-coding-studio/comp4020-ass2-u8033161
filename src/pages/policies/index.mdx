# Policies and support

## Late work

Work submitted after the deadline loses 5% of the available marks per day,
for up to five days; after five days it receives zero. This applies per
assessment, calculated from the exact due time on that assessment's page.

Extensions must be requested in writing at least 48 hours before the
deadline. Medical or compassionate grounds are the exception: apply as soon
as you reasonably can, with documentation, even after the deadline has
passed.

## Academic integrity

Submitted work must be yours. Discussing an approach with another student is
fine; submitting their solution, or a solution an AI tool produced for you
without disclosed and substantive editing, is not. Declare any assistance —
a person, a tool, an AI system — in your submission's process notes.

The same rule the course applies to its own content applies to yours: a
specific factual claim (a cost figure, a cited standard, a measured result)
must be traceable to a real source, or clearly labelled as an illustrative
example. An invented number presented as if it were measured is a fabrication,
in your work or in the course's.

## Participation

Labs are where marked feedback actually happens — a mark you get in a Lab is
faster and more specific than the same mark explained over email a week
later. Where a Lab asks you to bring something (a package, a set of
measurements), have it ready; the exercise doesn't work retroactively.

## Getting help

Ask in the Lab first — see [People](/people/) for who teaches which session
and how to reach them. Course-level questions (weighting, extensions, the
syllabus) go to the convenor by email; a mark you disagree with goes back to
whoever gave it, before it goes anywhere else.
