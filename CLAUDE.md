# CLAUDE.md — SLOP1486

## Sourcing and fabrication

Any specific factual claim in course content (a number, a standard, a formula, a statistic) must be traceable to a real source. Record it in that page's `spec:` frontmatter field, with enough detail (source name, and a link where practical) that someone could verify it independently.

Invented numbers used purely for teaching exercises (e.g. sample parcel dimensions for a costing exercise) are fine, but must never be listed in `spec:` and must be labeled inline as illustrative/example data — never presented as a real measured result. Do not let a drafted paragraph blur this line by presenting an invented figure as if it were a cited fact.

## Course thesis discipline

The course's throughline claim is: sustainability claims on packaging are largely marketing language; what actually drives packaging design decisions is shipping-cost formulas and return/liability cost. Every session and lecture must connect back to this claim somehow — either testing it, complicating it, or applying it to a new case. Reject or rewrite any agent-drafted content that reads as a self-contained topic with no connection to the throughline; a page that could be dropped into an unrelated course without edits has failed this rule.

## Difficulty progression

Weeks 1-6 build foundational vocabulary and tools (materials, test standards, cost formulas) at easy-to-medium difficulty. Weeks 7-12 apply that vocabulary to harder, more entangled real-world constraints (returns, psychology, international regulation, accessibility, integrative redesign) at medium-to-hard difficulty. A later week must assume and build on an earlier week's content, not restate it.

## Voice

Plain, direct register — no filler adjectives, no "in today's fast-paced world"-style throat-clearing. Prefer a concrete number or example over a vague claim. If a paragraph could have been written about any packaging-adjacent topic without change, rewrite it to be specific to this course's claim.
